Buying in small lots

We buy one container-size lot per origin, never more than we can roast in a season. The farmers we work with know exactly what we took, and we know exactly what came back — the trace is short on purpose.

Every bag carries the farm name, the washing station, and the harvest month. If we can’t print it, we don’t buy it.

Direct tradeTraceable lotsHarvest-dated

Roasting twelve kilos

A small drum roaster, one person on the dial, and a curve logged for every lot. Light-medium is where these coffees taste most like themselves; anything darker is a compromise we don’t make.

Tuesdays at nine. The drum is visible from the counter, so you can watch the batch you’ll drink later that afternoon.

12 kg batchesLogged curvesLight-medium

Resting five days

Fresh-roasted coffee is stubborn — the CO2 makes it foamy, sour, and uneven in the brew. We let every lot rest five days in valve-sealed bags before it reaches the bar, then cup it again to confirm it settled.

patience is part of the recipe

Brewing to order

Each morning we cup the day’s roast and dial the grind, dose, and pour for that specific lot. The recipe on the wall changes daily, because the coffee does. Nothing is brewed ahead and kept warm.

If a cup tastes wrong, tell us at the counter — we’ll brew another one and split the difference.

Daily dial-inV630 & espressoRecipe on the wall

Serving it in front of you

The whole process ends three metres from where it began: brew bar facing the roaster, mugs washed between cups, and a short note on the saucer about what you’re tasting. Sit down, it takes about three minutes.
